Hydrodynamic Marine Dynamics

Solunar Tidal Bite Windows: 4 Coastal Feeding Cycles

Fish are cold-blooded predators driven by water movement and atmospheric pressures. Position your tackle to intercept predatory migrations during these four distinct hydraulic phases.

Phase 01 Peak Strike Window

Incoming Flood Tide

First 2.5 hours after Low Slack

Surging ocean swells push nutrient-dense water into intertidal flats and estuary mouth channels. Baitfish are forced towards mangrove roots and shore gutters.

Target: Barramundi, Trevally
Lure: Surface Poppers & Jerkbaits
Phase 02 Vertical Window

High Water Slack

30 to 45 min pause at peak depth

Lateral current completely stalls as the tide reaches its highest elevation. Horizontal lures lose action. Large bottom predators emerge from deep rock crevices.

Target: Grouper, Mangrove Jack, Snapper
Lure: Slow Pitch Metal Jigs
Phase 03 Ambush Flush

Ebb Tide Flush

Hours 1.5 through 4 of outgoing water

Water rapidly drains from shallow mudbanks, sweeping crabs, juvenile mullet, and prawns into bottleneck rivermouths. Predators stack up on outer drop-offs.

Target: Threadfin Salmon, Queenfish
Lure: Deep-Diving Crankbaits
Phase 04 Tidal Amplitude

Spring vs Neap Tides

Full / New Moon vs Quarter Moons

Spring tides produce maximum water volume and violent rips; use spider wire sinkers. Neap tides offer gentle flows perfect for ultra-light finesse casting.

Target: All Marine Species

Coastal Stewardship & Conservation

The 8 Angler Commandments of Sustainable Catch & Release

HookPoint is committed to zero net harm to marine ecosystems. When landing trophy game fish, execute these field protocols to achieve a 98%+ post-release survival rate.

01

Wet Hands Only

Never handle a fish with dry skin or dry rags. Dry surfaces wipe away the external mucus shield, causing lethal fungal infections within 72 hours.

02

No Gill Contact

Never insert fingers under the gill plate. Gill filaments are micro-capillaries that bleed easily, causing rapid asphyxiation upon return.

03

Horizontal Body Support

Never hold a heavy fish vertically by the jaw gripper alone. Always cradle the belly horizontally to prevent internal organ separation.

04

Pliers-First De-Hooking

Always extract hooks with long-nose saltwater pliers. Never twist hooks with bare fingers, which damages the fish's cartilage.

05

Crush Treble Barbs

Crimp hook barbs flat with pliers. Barbless hooks penetrate deeper on hooksets and can be slipped free in under 5 seconds.

06

30-Second Air Limit

Limit out-of-water photo documentation to under thirty seconds. Hold your breath while the fish is out — when you need air, the fish is starving for it.

07

Active Upstream Revival

Face exhausted fish forward into the oncoming water current to pass oxygen through the gills. Never release until the fish vigorously kicks away.

08

Zero Micro-Plastic Waste

Clip discarded mono, fluorocarbon, and braid into your pocket disposal tube. Ghost line takes up to 600 years to decompose in ocean reefs.
